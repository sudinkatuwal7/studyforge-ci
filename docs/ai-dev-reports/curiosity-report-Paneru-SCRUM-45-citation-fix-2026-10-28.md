### AI
## Why Gemini, when we already have OpenAI?
Pandu uses two different AI models, one for each step:

| Step | Model | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Find the relevant chunks | **Gemini** (`gemini-embedding-2`) | Turns the question into a vector (768 numbers) and compares it with the stored vectors for each chunk to find the closest matches |
| 2. Write the answer | **OpenAI** (`gpt-4o-mini`) | Reads those chunks and writes the answer with citations |

Your OpenAI key only covers step 2. Step 1 needs the question embedded by **the same model** that embedded the document chunks. Vectors from different embedding models aren't comparable, much like two maps drawn on different coordinate systems. OpenAI's embeddings also have a different length (1536 or 3072 numbers), so they wouldn't even fit in the database column.

Moving everything to OpenAI would mean changing the embedding code and re-embedding every document. The task explicitly lists "embedding logic" as out of scope. So a Gemini key is required as long as Pandu runs.

## Why that specific PDF?
- **To reproduce the reported bug.** The bad citation happened on the Hazard Management Costs passage in that PDF. Running the same question on the same passage is the most direct proof that the fix handles the real case. Any other PDF only shows that Pandu works in general.
- **Any PDF still needs Gemini.** Uploading a document runs it through processing, which embeds the chunks with Gemini. Without a Gemini key, no new document becomes searchable.

The model also doesn't make the citation mistake on every run. Even with the right PDF, the live run may produce a correct citation on the first try, and then the repair step never triggers. That's why the unit tests matter more here: they force the bad-then-good and bad-then-bad cases every time. The live check confirms the whole flow on real data; it doesn't guarantee the repair path runs.
